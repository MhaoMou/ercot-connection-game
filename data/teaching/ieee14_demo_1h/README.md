# IEEE 14-bus teaching demo (one-hour periods)

**This is a teaching demo. It is not the game network.** Scores, prices and
hosting capacities here say nothing about the ACTIVSg2000 scenarios used in
the competition. The package exists so that students (or an AI agent demo)
can run the whole student workflow in seconds:

```bash
A=data/teaching/ieee14_demo_1h
R=data/teaching/dispatch_scoring_rules_ieee14_demo_1h_v1.json

ercot-game-hosting-capacity --artifact-dir $A --bus-id 13 --bus-id 14 \
  --start-period 157 --period-count 1 --output-dir work/demo_hc --write-long-form
ercot-game-check-csv --file plans/team-a.csv --artifact-dir $A
ercot-game-preview --submissions-dir plans --artifact-dir $A --rules $R \
  --horizon 168 --preview-stride 1 --submission-number 2 --output work/demo_preview.json
ercot-game-batch run --submissions-dir plans --artifact-dir $A --rules $R \
  --output-dir work/demo_batch --horizon 168 --time-step-hours 1
```

Use `--preview-stride 1` with this package. It solves all 168 hours in well
under a second. The default stride of 6 samples only 28 hours (0, 6, ...,
162). That sample includes hour 156, next to the peak, but skips the peak
hour 157.

## Package contract

- `package_version`: `ieee14-demo-1h`, one block `ieee14_demo_week`.
- Horizon: 168 one-hour periods (one week, 24 periods per day; period 0 is
  00:00-01:00 on day 1). `time_step_hours = 1`. Storage `daily_exact_cycle`
  uses 24-period days, because the evaluator computes 24 / time_step_hours.
- Bus IDs are `"1"` to `"14"`; bus 1 is the reference bus.
- Per-hour loads are in `demand_bus.csv`. The package does not use the
  half-year calendar.
- Every file is regenerated by `python -m tools.build_ieee14_demo_package`.
  The builder checks each hour with a DCOPF (HiGHS), records the file hashes
  in `manifest.json` and also writes the demo scoring rules. It does not
  touch this README.

## How the numbers were chosen

**Canonical IEEE-14 data:** topology (20 branches), branch reactances, the
bus active-power loads (259 MW in total) and the generator locations and
nameplates (bus 1: 332.4 MW, bus 2: 140 MW, buses 3, 6 and 8: 100 MW each).

**Load shape:** this is the same mechanism the ACTIVSg2000 packages use. Each
base load is multiplied by `ercot_game.scenario_profiles.demand_multiplier`
for the first 168 hours of the 2028 `jul_dec` block. The values are hourly
and are not averaged.

- The multipliers run from 0.938 to 1.111. They are listed in
  `manifest.json`.
- Total load ranges from 243 to 288 MW.
- The profile week is 1-7 July 2028, Saturday to Friday.
- The peak is hour 157: day 7, 13:00-14:00.

**Generator costs:** these are linear instructional values. The canonical
case uses quadratic costs and has condensers at buses 3, 6 and 8. The costs
rise with distance from the slack bus and stay in the ACTIVSg2000 range of
0-29 $/MWh:

| generator | bus | Pmax (MW) | cost ($/MWh) |
|---|---|---|---|
| g1 | 1 | 332.4 | 14 |
| g2 | 2 | 140 | 18 |
| g3 | 3 | 100 | 24 |
| g6 | 6 | 100 | 27 |
| g8 | 8 | 100 | 30 |

When no line is congested, g1 serves all load and every LMP is 14 $/MWh.
When lines bind, LMPs separate. For example, with about 26 MW added at both
bus 13 and bus 14 near the peak, the LMP is about 37 $/MWh at bus 13 and 72
$/MWh at bus 14.

**Thermal limits:** the canonical case has no ratings, so these MVA limits
are instructional choices. Lines among buses 1-5 and 7-9 have 1.3-2x
headroom over the peak base flow. The feeders on the low-voltage side
(buses 6 and 9-14) are tight. Lines 6-13, 9-14 and 13-14 feed buses 13 and
14 and form a shared bottleneck.

| branch | limit | branch | limit | branch | limit | branch | limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | 250 | 2-5 | 80 | 4-9 | 40 | 7-8 | 100 |
| 1-5 | 130 | 3-4 | 70 | 5-6 | 80 | 7-9 | 80 |
| 2-3 | 120 | 4-5 | 120 | 6-11 | 30 | 9-10 | 30 |
| 2-4 | 100 | 4-7 | 70 | 6-12 | 30 | 9-14 | 30 |
| 6-13 | 45 | 10-11 | 25 | 12-13 | 20 | 13-14 | 25 |

## Hosting capacity in the peak hour (157)

Hosting capacity is the largest extra load at one bus, on its own. Hour 157
is the minimum over the week at every bus:

| bus | MW | bus | MW | bus | MW |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 484.7 | 6 | 171.7 | 11 | 46.1 |
| 2 | 440.6 | 7 | 180.0 | 12 | 43.2 |
| 3 | 185.4 | 8 | 180.0 | 13 | 52.5 |
| 4 | 248.1 | 9 | 105.7 | 14 | 38.5 |
| 5 | 317.9 | 10 | 45.0 | | |

Buses 13 and 14 share the bottleneck. Line 9-14 binds at the hosting limit of
both buses.

- 20 MW at bus 13 plus 20 MW at bus 14 fits.
- Either 30 MW request fits on its own, but 30 MW at bus 13 plus 30 MW at bus
  14 does not fit. In the batch evaluator, the uniform rule then gives both
  teams 26.25 MW.

## Scoring rules

`dispatch_scoring_rules_ieee14_demo_1h_v1.json` is derived from the released
`dispatch_scoring_rules_jul_dec_6h_v1.json`, with `time_step_hours = 1`. The
operating coefficients are unchanged:

- IT value: 6500 $/MWh, constant over 168 periods
- generator cost curve: 80/150/250 $/MWh
- storage mode: `daily_exact_cycle`

The capital costs are prorated by 168/4416 hours (the jul_dec half-year), so
that one demo week has the same economics as one half-year submission of the
game:

- site cost: 25 M$/MW x 168/4416 = about 0.95 M$/MW
- generator cost: 1.5 M$/MW x 168/4416 = about 57 k$/MW
- storage cost: (400 k$/MW + 250 k$/MWh) x 168/4416

A fully served site then earns about 1.09 M$ per MW in the demo week (6500 x
168) against 0.95 M$/MW of site cost. It therefore breaks even at about 87%
served, the same as a site added in Submission 2 of the game.

In the projection example above, each team is served 26.25/30 = 87.5%. Both
teams then score slightly below zero, because they also pay for energy.

As in the game, an infeasible score of 0 ranks above a feasible plan with a
negative score.
