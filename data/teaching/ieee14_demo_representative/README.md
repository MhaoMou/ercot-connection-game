# IEEE 14-bus teaching demo (representative days)

**This is a teaching demo. It is not the game network.** Scores, prices and
hosting capacities here say nothing about the ACTIVSg2000 network used in
the competition. The package has the same format as the released
representative-day package, so students (or an AI agent demo) can run the
whole student workflow in seconds:

```bash
A=data/teaching/ieee14_demo_representative
R=data/teaching/dispatch_scoring_rules_ieee14_demo_representative_v1.json

ercot-game-hosting-capacity --artifact-dir $A --bus-id 13 --bus-id 14 \
  --start-period 109 --period-count 1 --output-dir work/demo_hc --write-long-form
ercot-game-check-csv --file plans/team-a.csv --artifact-dir $A
ercot-game-preview --submissions-dir plans --artifact-dir $A --rules $R \
  --week 2 --output work/demo_preview.json
ercot-game-batch run --submissions-dir plans --artifact-dir $A --rules $R \
  --week 1 --output-dir work/demo_s1
# Submission 2 runs on base load + the Submission 1 net withdrawal:
ercot-game-batch run --submissions-dir plans_s2 --artifact-dir $A --rules $R \
  --week 1 --submission-number 2 --base-load-from work/demo_s1 --output-dir work/demo_s2
```

All 120 periods solve in well under a second, so the defaults (stride 1)
are fine.

## Package contract

- `package_version`: `ieee14-demo-representative-v1`, one block
  `representative`, validated like `activsg2000-representative-v1`.
- Horizon: 5 representative days x 24 one-hour periods = 120 periods
  (`time_step_hours = 1`). Period `24 d + h` is hour `h` of day `d`.
  Storage `daily_exact_cycle` uses 24-period days.
- Days and weights are those of the main package:

| day | date | weight (days/year) | peak period |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2028-01-03 | 83.770 | 13 |
| 1 | 2028-05-10 | 99.726 | 37 |
| 2 | 2028-07-12 | 82.773 | 61 |
| 3 | 2028-09-10 | 97.732 | 85 |
| 4 | 2028-09-15 (peak day) | 1.000 | 109 |

- Bus IDs are `"1"` to `"14"`; bus 1 is the reference bus.
- Per-hour loads are in `demand_bus.csv`.
- Every file except this README is regenerated by
  `python -m tools.build_ieee14_demo_package`. The builder reads the days
  and weights from `data/processed/activsg2000_representative_v1`, checks
  each period with a DCOPF (HiGHS), records the file hashes in
  `manifest.json` and also writes the demo scoring rules.

## How the numbers were chosen

**Canonical IEEE-14 data:** topology (20 branches), branch reactances, the
bus active-power loads (259 MW in total) and the generator locations and
nameplates (bus 1: 332.4 MW, bus 2: 140 MW, buses 3, 6 and 8: 100 MW each).

**Load shape:** the same mechanism as the main package. Each base load is
multiplied by `ercot_game.scenario_profiles.demand_multiplier` for the same
day and hour of 2028 (hour `block_offset(2028, block) + source_period_start
+ h`). The multipliers run from 0.867 to 1.165 (listed in `manifest.json`);
total load ranges from 224 to 302 MW. The peak is period 109 (peak day,
13:00-14:00).

**Generator costs:** linear instructional values (the canonical case uses
quadratic costs and has condensers at buses 3, 6 and 8). Costs rise with
distance from the slack bus:

| generator | bus | Pmax (MW) | cost ($/MWh) |
|---|---|---|---|
| g1 | 1 | 332.4 | 14 |
| g2 | 2 | 140 | 18 |
| g3 | 3 | 100 | 24 |
| g6 | 6 | 100 | 27 |
| g8 | 8 | 100 | 30 |

When no line is congested, g1 serves all load and every LMP is 14 $/MWh.
When lines bind, LMPs separate.

**Thermal limits:** the canonical case has no ratings, so these MVA limits
are instructional choices. Lines among buses 1-5 and 7-9 have headroom over
the peak base flow; the low-voltage feeders (buses 6 and 9-14) are tight.
Lines 6-13, 9-14 and 13-14 feed buses 13 and 14 and form a shared
bottleneck.

| branch | limit | branch | limit | branch | limit | branch | limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | 250 | 2-5 | 80 | 4-9 | 40 | 7-8 | 100 |
| 1-5 | 130 | 3-4 | 70 | 5-6 | 80 | 7-9 | 80 |
| 2-3 | 120 | 4-5 | 120 | 6-11 | 30 | 9-10 | 30 |
| 2-4 | 100 | 4-7 | 70 | 6-12 | 30 | 9-14 | 30 |
| 6-13 | 45 | 10-11 | 25 | 12-13 | 20 | 13-14 | 25 |

## Hosting capacity in the peak hour (period 109)

Hosting capacity is the largest extra load at one bus, on its own. Period
109 is the minimum over all 120 periods at every bus:

| bus | MW | bus | MW | bus | MW |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 470.7 | 6 | 168.8 | 11 | 45.4 |
| 2 | 428.8 | 7 | 177.7 | 12 | 42.9 |
| 3 | 180.3 | 8 | 177.7 | 13 | 50.8 |
| 4 | 240.9 | 9 | 103.0 | 14 | 37.6 |
| 5 | 310.8 | 10 | 44.5 | | |

Buses 13 and 14 share the bottleneck:

- 20 MW at bus 13 plus 20 MW at bus 14 fits.
- Either 30 MW request fits on its own, but 30 MW at bus 13 plus 30 MW at bus
  14 does not. The batch evaluator's uniform rule then gives both teams
  about 25.4 MW.

## Scoring rules

`dispatch_scoring_rules_ieee14_demo_representative_v1.json` is exactly the
main release economics (`dispatch-scoring-representative-v1`, built by
`scripts/create_representative_scoring_rules.py`) with this package's day
weights, which are the same as the main package's:

- IT value 1000 $/MWh; every energy term of period t is weighted by
  `1 h x day weight x A`, `A = sum_{y=1..10} 1.07^-y = 7.024`
- capital charged once, undiscounted: site 25 M$/MW, generator 2.0 M$/MW,
  storage 400 k$/MW + 250 k$/MWh
- generator capacity at most 0.5 x connection capacity, at most 2 hours of
  full output per day; cost curve 80/150/250 $/MWh
- storage mode `daily_exact_cycle`

A site breaks even at about 40.6% served (25e6 / (1000 x 8760 x A)).
Reported served load is MWh per represented year.

As in the game, an infeasible score of 0 ranks above a feasible plan with a
negative score.
